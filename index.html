ThinkPad T470 Field Guide

Specs, upgrades and care for Lenovo's 14-inch business laptop with the swappable rear battery.

TYU GHJ BNM
Introduction

The ThinkPad T470 is a 14-inch business laptop built on Intel's 6th and 7th generation Core U-series processors. It keeps the things T-series owners care about: two RAM slots, a full set of ports, a TrackPoint with three physical buttons, and Lenovo's Power Bridge system with an internal battery plus a removable rear one.

That mix makes it a popular used laptop. It's easy to open, parts are cheap, and Lenovo publishes a hardware maintenance manual (HMM) that shows every screw, cable and part number. Use the HMM as the real reference when you take yours apart.

Checks before you buy used

  • Ask for battery health as a percentage of design capacity for both batteries, not just their age
  • Make sure no BIOS supervisor password is set. It can't easily be removed.
  • Check which screen it has. The HD panel is much dimmer and narrower than the FHD IPS one.
  • Look up the machine type on the sticker underneath in PSREF to see its exact processor, screen and options
Processor and Graphics

Every T470 processor is a dual-core, four-thread Intel Core U-series chip with DDR4-2133 memory support. The 7th generation (Kaby Lake) chips run a little faster and have better video decoding, so they're the ones to look for.

Graphics

  • Most models use only the integrated Intel HD Graphics 520 or 620
  • Some models add an NVIDIA GeForce 940MX with 2 GB of GDDR5 memory. These aren't sold in EMEA.
  • Up to three independent displays, connected through HDMI or USB Type-C
  • Maximum external resolution is 4096x2304 at 60 Hz over USB Type-C and 4096x2160 at 24 Hz over HDMI

To see which processor and GPU your machine has on Linux:

lscpu | grep "Model name"
lspci | grep -E "VGA|3D"
Memory and Storage

The T470 has two DDR4 SO-DIMM sockets and no soldered memory, so all of it can be upgraded. Lenovo lists a maximum of 32 GB at 2400 MHz, dual-channel capable. The processors run memory at DDR4-2133, so faster sticks work but clock down.

Choosing RAM

  • Use DDR4 SO-DIMM laptop memory, 2133 MHz or faster
  • Fit two identical sticks. Dual channel can nearly double integrated graphics speed.
  • Two 16 GB sticks reach the official 32 GB maximum

Storage options

The main drive bay takes either a 2.5-inch drive or an M.2 2280 SSD, not both at once.

Drive Details
2.5" HDD SATA 6.0 Gb/s, 7 mm high, with Active Protection System
2.5" SSD SATA 6.0 Gb/s, 7 mm high
M.2 2280 SSD PCIe NVMe, PCIe 3.0 x2, 16 Gb/s
Second drive 128 GB M.2 SATA SSD in the WWAN slot. Can't be combined with WWAN or WiGig.

The M.2 slot runs at PCIe 3.0 x2, so a fast x4 NVMe drive works but tops out around half its rated speed. There is no optical drive.

Before you open the case: turn off the built-in battery in the BIOS under Config, Power, and remove the rear battery. The laptop won't turn on again until you plug in the charger, so nothing is live while you work.

Step by step

  1. Shut down, unplug the charger, disable the built-in battery and pull out the rear battery
  2. Loosen the bottom cover screws. They stay attached to the cover.
  3. Pry the cover off with a plastic tool, not a screwdriver
  4. Swap the part. RAM goes in at an angle and clicks down. An M.2 SSD goes in at an angle and is held by one screw.
  5. Close it up and plug in the charger to wake it
  6. The first boot after a RAM change may show a black screen for a minute while it trains the memory. Let it finish.

Check what's installed and how healthy it is on Linux:

sudo dmidecode -t memory | grep -E "Size|Speed|Type:"
lsblk -o NAME,SIZE,MODEL
sudo smartctl -a /dev/nvme0 | grep -E "Percentage Used|Data Units Written"
Display

All T470 screens are 14.0-inch, 16:9, anti-glare panels with LED backlights. Three panels were offered, and the difference between them is big.

Panel Brightness Contrast Notes
HD 1366x768 220 nits 400:1 Low resolution, narrow viewing angles
FHD 1920x1080 220 nits 400:1 or 500:1 Sharper, still not IPS
FHD IPS 1920x1080 250 nits 700:1 170° viewing angle. Optional 10-finger on-cell touch.

The FHD IPS panel is the one to get. Touch is only offered on the FHD IPS panel with the PPS display cover. On Linux you can check the panel model with: 

sudo get-edid | parse-edid | grep ModelName
Ports and Wireless

The T470 doesn't need dongles for everyday use. Thunderbolt 3 on the Type-C port handles charging, displays and fast storage.

Ports

  • Three USB 3.1 Gen 1 (USB 3.0), one Always On
  • One USB 3.1 Type-C Gen 2 / Thunderbolt 3 with Power Delivery and DisplayPort
  • HDMI 1.4b
  • Ethernet (RJ-45)
  • Dock connector
  • 4-in-1 reader (MMC, SD, SDHC, SDXC), SD 3.0 with UHS-I
  • Combo audio and microphone jack
  • Optional smart card reader

For VGA projectors, Lenovo sells an HDMI to VGA adapter and a USB-C to VGA adapter. An optional ThinkPad WiGig dock connects wirelessly.

Wired and wireless networking

Ethernet Intel I219-V (non-vPro) or I219-LM (vPro)
Wi-Fi One M.2 card: Realtek RTL8822BE, Intel 8260, Intel 8265 or Intel Tri-Band 18265 with WiGig. All are 2x2 802.11ac.
Bluetooth 4.1, built into the Wi-Fi card. The Intel 8265 and 18265 support 4.2 but Windows limits them to 4.1.
WWAN Optional 4G LTE-A (Sierra Wireless EM7455) or 4G LTE (Fibocom L831-EAU, EMEA only)
SIM Micro-SIM slot on WWAN models
NFC Optional

Intel Wi-Fi cards have the best Linux support. If yours came with the Realtek card, swapping it for an Intel 8265 is a cheap upgrade.

Camera and audio

  • Optional 720p camera, or an IR plus 720p camera for Windows Hello (not with WiGig)
  • Realtek ALC3268 codec, 2 W x 2 stereo speakers, dual array microphones
Battery and Power

The T470 uses Power Bridge: an internal 3-cell 24 Wh battery plus a removable rear battery that's either 3-cell 24 Wh or 6-cell 48 Wh or 72 Wh. You can swap the rear battery while the laptop is running. The laptop drains the rear one first, so it wears faster and is the cheaper one to replace.

Battery life (MobileMark 2014)

Batteries Integrated GPU Discrete GPU
3-cell 6.9 hr 6.9 hr
3 + 3-cell 13.9 hr 13.9 hr
6-cell 48 Wh 13.9 hr 14.3 hr
6-cell 72 Wh 20.9 hr 21.6 hr
3 + 6-cell 48 Wh 20.9 hr 21.2 hr
3 + 6-cell 72 Wh 27.1 hr 28.6 hr

These are lab numbers. Real-world battery life is much lower and depends on screen brightness and what you run. The 72 Wh rear battery sticks out underneath but is the best upgrade for battery life.

Chargers

The T470 charges from a 45 W or 65 W slim-tip adapter, or a 45 W or 65 W USB Type-C adapter on the Thunderbolt port. Use 65 W on models with the NVIDIA GPU.

Check battery health

Compare each battery's current full capacity with its design capacity. Under 80% means it's worn. On Windows: 

powercfg /batteryreport

On Linux, the internal battery is BAT0 and the rear one is BAT1: 

upower -i /org/freedesktop/UPower/devices/battery_BAT0
upower -i /org/freedesktop/UPower/devices/battery_BAT1

Set a charge limit

Batteries wear fastest when they sit full and warm. Capping the charge around 80% slows that down a lot. On Windows, use Lenovo Vantage under Power. On Linux, set it for both batteries: 

echo 75 | sudo tee /sys/class/power_supply/BAT{0,1}/charge_control_start_threshold
echo 80 | sudo tee /sys/class/power_supply/BAT{0,1}/charge_control_end_threshold

These reset on reboot, so use TLP (covered in Linux and Firmware) to keep them.

Keyboard and Security

The T470 has a 6-row, spill-resistant keyboard with multimedia Fn keys and an optional LED backlight. Below it is the UltraNav: a TrackPoint with three physical buttons and a buttonless Mylar multi-touch touchpad.

Shortcuts

  • Fn + Esc toggles FnLock, which swaps media keys and F1 to F12
  • Fn + Space cycles the keyboard backlight on models that have one
  • Fn and Ctrl can be swapped in the BIOS under Config, Keyboard/Mouse
  • Hold the middle TrackPoint button and push to scroll

The keyboard is cheap to replace and comes out from the top after removing a couple of screws. Check the HMM for the exact steps.

Security features

  • Power-on, hard disk and supervisor passwords, plus a security keyhole
  • Discrete TPM 1.2 or 2.0, TCG certified
  • Optional Intel vPro on supported processors
  • Optional touch fingerprint reader with match-on-chip, on the keyboard bezel
  • Optional smart card reader and NFC
Linux and Firmware

The T470 runs Linux well. The Intel chipset, graphics and Wi-Fi cards are supported out of the box, and the thinkpad_acpi driver handles the Fn keys, fan and both batteries.

Power management with TLP

TLP tunes power use and keeps your charge limits across reboots. Turn off power-profiles-daemon first, since it conflicts with TLP: 

sudo apt install tlp tlp-rdw
sudo systemctl mask power-profiles-daemon
sudo systemctl enable --now tlp
sudo tlp-stat -b

Then add the thresholds for both batteries to /etc/tlp.conf and run sudo tlp start: 

START_CHARGE_THRESH_BAT0=75
STOP_CHARGE_THRESH_BAT0=80
START_CHARGE_THRESH_BAT1=75
STOP_CHARGE_THRESH_BAT1=80

BIOS

Press F1 at power-on for BIOS setup or F12 for the boot menu. Enter shows the startup menu with all options.

  • Config, Power: disable the built-in battery before repairs
  • Config, Keyboard/Mouse: swap Fn and Ctrl, and set the F1 to F12 default
  • Security, Virtualization: turn on VT-x for virtual machines and WSL
  • Startup: set the boot order and choose UEFI only

Updating firmware

Install BIOS and Thunderbolt firmware updates, since they fix security holes and hardware bugs. On Linux, use LVFS: 

fwupdmgr refresh
fwupdmgr get-updates
fwupdmgr update

On Windows, use Lenovo Vantage or download the update from Lenovo's support site. Plug in the charger first and never turn the laptop off while an update runs.
